Control Without Presence

Mars Avatar Project is an open reference architecture for supervising fleets of autonomous robotic avatars operating under full Earth–Mars communication latency.

White Paper v3.6 (PDF) GitHub Repository DOI Record
Permanent Concept DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21185807

Project Overview

Surface operations on Mars are severely constrained by light-time communication delays (4 to 24 minutes one-way). Direct teleoperation is physics-impossible, while scripted command sequencing creates high operational overhead and low situational throughput. Mars Avatar Project proposes a high-level supervisory model shifting human operators from direct control to strategic intent specification.

Key Architecture Contributions

  • Supervisory Control Model De-couples operator decision cycles from physical execution loops through intent-driven mission directives.
  • Mission AI Orchestration Centralized autonomous coordination layer handling real-time task decomposition, dynamic scheduling, and failure recovery.
  • Heterogeneous Fleet Coordination Unified abstraction protocols enabling seamless task allocation across highly specialized robotic avatars.
  • Earth–Mars Latency Architecture Dual-state world modeling ensuring robust local autonomy under intermittent or highly asymmetrical communication channels.

The Central Coordination Layer: Mission AI

Mission AI represents the primary original contribution of the Mars Avatar framework. Functioning as an intermediary autonomous system deployed on surface local compute nodes (e.g., lander or habitat server), Mission AI resolves high-level strategic objectives into validated execution plans for individual robotic units.

Shared World Model

Maintains dynamic 3D semantic maps, environmental state estimations, and fleet spatial topology updated asynchronously.

Task Decomposition & Allocation

Translates complex scientific goals into sub-tasks assigned based on robot capability, power budget, and proximity.

Fault Management & Degradation

Executes deterministic degradation hierarchies when individual units encounter mechanical failure or loss of signal.

Comm Scheduling

Optimizes orbital relay passes and direct-to-Earth uplink windows to maximize payload data throughput.

v3.6 Reference Fleet Classification

Scientific Rover

Primary Geology & Biosignatures

Equipped with high-precision analytical instruments, core sampling drills, and micro-imaging suites.

Construction Robot

In-Situ Infrastructure

Heavy-duty manipulator arms designed for regolith grading, shielding assembly, and structural anchor placement.

Cargo Robot

Material Transport & Logistics

High-payload mobility platform optimized for autonomous haulage of raw regolith, toolpacks, and core samples.

Maintenance Robot

Asset Servicing & Inspection

Fine-dexterity manipulation unit tasked with dust mitigation, solar panel clearing, and electrical interface checks.

Laboratory Unit

Mobile Analysis Facility

Stationary or semi-mobile centralized laboratory node housing sealed mass spectrometers and sample storage arrays.

Reconnaissance Drone

Aerial Mapping & Scouting

Rotorcraft unit performing high-speed visual-thermal mapping, terrain elevation modeling, and line-of-sight relays.

Research Context & State of the Art

Dimension Traditional Mars Operations Contemporary Multi-Agent Work Mars Avatar Extension
Control Paradigm Direct waypoint / scripted command sequence sent per diurnal cycle. Localized swarm autonomy or scripted master-slave pairs. Intent-driven supervisory control via surface-hosted Mission AI.
Latency Resilience Execution halts on ambiguity; requires Earth ground crew intervention. Short time-horizon local obstacle avoidance. Multi-hour task autonomy with dynamic reallocation upon unit fault.
Fleet Integration Single-rover isolated operation or independent dual-rover missions. Homogeneous robot swarms in structured environments. Heterogeneous multi-role fleet sharing a single dynamic world model.

Research Roadmap

PHASE 01

Architecture Specification & Formalization

Objective: Define math and protocol abstractions for latency-tolerant intent handling.

Deliverables: White Paper v3.6, mathematical formulation of degradation hierarchies.

Status: Completed / Published.

PHASE 02

Simulation Environment & Software-in-the-Loop

Objective: Build Gazebo/ROS2 simulated environment replicating Martian topography and relay delays.

Deliverables: Open-source ROS2 packages for Mission AI orchestration testbed.

Potential Collaborators: Academic robotics laboratories, ROS community research groups.

PHASE 03

Analogue Field Validation

Objective: Test multi-unit coordination under simulated latency in Earth-analog terrain (e.g., Iceland/Atacama).

Deliverables: Empirical benchmarking data on operator cognitive load and mission throughput.

Potential Collaborators: Planetary science research institutions and space agencies.

Known Limitations & Scientific Transparency

In accordance with rigorous academic standards, the Mars Avatar Project explicitly states the current boundaries and unvalidated assumptions of this work:

  • Conceptual Stage: The architecture is currently a theoretical and algorithmic model. Physical validation under spaceflight conditions has not occurred.
  • Compute Assumptions: Mission AI assumes high-reliability rad-hard edge computing available on planetary landers, which currently exceeds standard flight-proven hardware limits.
  • Communication Bounds: Extreme solar conjunctions (complete loss of telemetry for ~2 weeks) require total pre-planned autonomous standby protocols not fully detailed in v3.6.
