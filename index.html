Definition

What This Is — and What It Is Not

Stated before anything else, because the value of a concept study depends on it being read as one.

This project is

  • A reference architecture
  • A research concept, published in full
  • An open publication with a permanent DOI
  • Independent work by a single author
  • An open invitation to review, challenge, and collaborate

This project is not

  • Flight hardware
  • Operational software
  • A NASA, ESA, or other agency mission
  • A commercial product
  • A validated engineering system

No hardware has been built, and no component of the architecture has been experimentally validated. References to prior work are provided for scientific context only and do not imply affiliation or endorsement.

System Design

System Architecture

Six stages between intent and result. Every interface is specified around one fixed condition: the one-way signal delay between Earth and Mars is 4 to 22 minutes, and no part of the chain may assume a real-time response.

01

Human Operator

Defines objectives, constraints, priorities, and acceptance criteria. Retains authority over every irreversible action.

02

Mission AI

Translates objectives into executable tasks, allocates them across the fleet, monitors execution, and manages faults within its assigned decision class.

03

Deep Space Communication

Uplink and downlink scheduled against limited windows. Traffic is prioritized by information value rather than by request order.

04

Mars Relay Network

Orbital and surface relays extend local coverage and buffer traffic between contact periods.

05

Autonomous Robotic Fleet

Specialized avatars execute tasks on the surface, respond to hazards within seconds, and negotiate reallocation among themselves.

06

Scientific Operations

Measurements, samples, and constructed infrastructure, returned with provenance and the decision record that produced them.

Authority flows downward; evidence flows back on the same delayed channel. The operator reviews outcomes and authorizes the next objective — the operator never steers. Mission AI is the layer that makes this practical: it holds the fleet's shared model of the mission and decides how work is distributed when Earth cannot be consulted in time.

Coordination Layer

Mission AI

Mission AI is the principal original contribution of this work. Goal-based commanding, onboard navigation autonomy, and supervisory telepresence each exist independently; what has not been specified is the layer that holds a fleet of them coherent while the operator is 4 to 22 minutes out of date. White Paper v3.6 defines it as a five-layer reference architecture with a formal goal-package protocol, graduated decision classes, and measurable quality metrics.

01

Mission Coordination

Holds the active goal set, resolves conflicts between concurrent objectives, and maintains a single authoritative plan for the fleet.

02

Shared World Model

A common representation of terrain, assets, resources, and uncertainty, kept consistent across units that observe different parts of it at different times.

03

Task Decomposition

Expands an operator goal package into ordered, verifiable subtasks with explicit preconditions and success criteria.

04

Task Allocation

Assigns subtasks across a heterogeneous fleet using decentralized market-based methods (CBBA and successors), tolerating intermittent links between units.

05

Fleet Synchronization

Keeps dependent activities aligned in time and space where two or more units must cooperate on a single task.

06

Execution Monitoring

Compares observed progress against the plan and detects divergence early enough to act locally, rather than at the next Earth window.

07

Fault Management

Detects, isolates, and contains failures; re-plans around a lost or degraded unit instead of halting the mission.

08

Degradation Hierarchy

An ordered set of reduced-capability modes, so that the system loses throughput rather than the mission when conditions worsen.

09

Communication Scheduling

Prioritizes and queues uplink and downlink traffic against limited, intermittent windows and finite relay bandwidth.

Central Claim

A coordinating fleet does not remove the boundary of the modelled fault space. It converts a class of mission-loss risks into throughput losses, while leaving correlated (common-mode) failures untouched.

Stated narrowly and deliberately, so that it can be tested and refuted.

Authority boundary: Mission AI does not set mission priorities. Which site to investigate and which sample to collect remain human decisions. The system is trusted to execute safely, not to choose objectives.

Design Rationale

Why MAP?

Six properties that distinguish this architecture from single-vehicle remote operation.

Human-in-the-Loop

Every decision of consequence remains with the operator; only the steering is delegated.

Mission AI Coordination

A single coordination layer holds the shared world model and allocates work across the fleet.

Goal-Based Control

Earth transmits the objective and its constraints; the fleet determines the method on site.

Fleet Intelligence

Specialized units bid for tasks and reassign them when a unit degrades or a plan is invalidated.

Scalable Architecture

Layer interfaces are defined independently of unit count, so the fleet can grow without redesign.

Earth → Moon → Mars Roadmap

A staged verification sequence with explicit criteria for progressing from one environment to the next.

Hardware Concept

Robotic Avatar Fleet

Rather than a single general-purpose rover, the architecture assumes a small heterogeneous fleet whose units are individually simpler and collectively redundant. The composition below follows White Paper v3.6.

01 · Mobility · Science

Scientific Rover

Long-range traverse, in-situ measurement, sample acquisition, and site characterization ahead of and alongside other units.

02 · Mobility · Manipulation

Construction Robot

Surface preparation and assembly: landing pads, foundations, and deployment of structures and power infrastructure.

03 · Mobility · Logistics

Cargo Robot

Transport of samples, consumables, tools, and components between work sites, caches, and the base area.

04 · Mobility · Servicing

Maintenance Robot

Inspection, cleaning, component replacement, and recovery of degraded units — the fleet's means of extending its own service life.

05 · Stationary · Analysis

Laboratory Unit

Fixed or slowly relocatable analysis facility for sample processing and instrumentation too heavy or too sensitive to carry on a traverse.

06 · Aerial · Survey

Reconnaissance Drone

Short-duration aerial survey for route scouting, hazard identification, and rapid coverage of terrain that ground units cannot reach.

All units are conceptual. None has been designed at hardware level; mass, power, thermal, and radiation design are outside the scope of the current document.

Development Sequence

Technology Roadmap

Indicative phasing. No phase is funded, scheduled, or under way; each is contingent on the results of the one before it.

2026–2030

Concept Demonstration

Simulation of the full command chain under injected latency, with reproducible benchmarks.

Objective
Model the goal package → coordination → execution pipeline under realistic delay and communication windows.
Deliverables
Open simulation environment, scenario set, and measured values for the quality metrics defined in the White Paper.
Expected outcome
First quantitative test of the central claim — whether coordination converts mission-loss risk into throughput loss.
Potential collaborators
Robotics and multi-agent systems research groups; simulation and ROS communities.
2030–2035

Lunar Demonstration

Supervisory command exercised on real hardware at genuine, if short, light-time delay.

Objective
Exercise supervisory command on real hardware at genuine, if short, light-time delay.
Deliverables
Reproducible experiment protocol, operator workload and throughput measurements, published results including negative results.
Expected outcome
Evidence on sim-to-real transfer and on failure modes that simulation does not surface.
Potential collaborators
University laboratories, lunar analog programmes, agency technology demonstrators.
2035–2045

Mars Deployment

The validated architecture applied to surface operations at full interplanetary delay.

Objective
Apply the validated architecture to surface operations at full interplanetary delay.
Deliverables
Flight-relevant architecture specification with transition criteria satisfied at each preceding stage.
Expected outcome
A coordination layer whose behaviour under delay has been characterised rather than assumed.
Potential collaborators
Space agencies, aerospace institutes, and industry partners.
Objective

Robotic Infrastructure

Landing pads, power, and stored consumables emplaced and maintained without a crew present.

Objective

Support for Human Missions

Routine surface work carried by supervised avatars, reserving crew time for what requires people.

Scientific Context

Related Work

The Mars Avatar Project builds on three decades of research in supervisory control, crew-operated telerobotics, and flight-proven rover autonomy.

  • Sheridan, T.B. (1992). Telerobotics, Automation, and Human Supervisory Control. MIT Press. The foundational text on supervisory control — humans working through semi-autonomous machines rather than steering them directly. The theoretical basis for the operator-to-supervisor shift at the core of this project. MIT Press →
  • NASA Surface Telerobotics (2013). Astronauts aboard the ISS remotely supervised the K10 rover at NASA Ames across four simulated mission phases — the first fully interactive operation of a planetary rover by a crew member in space, using command sequencing rather than joystick control. Paper (arXiv) →
  • ESA METERON / Analog-1 (2019–2022). A multi-year ESA campaign in which astronauts operated the Interact rover from orbit and in lunar-analog terrain on Mount Etna, testing force-feedback control under injected signal delay. ESA →
  • Verma et al. (2023). Autonomous robotics is driving Perseverance rover's progress on Mars. Science Robotics. Flight-proven evidence that onboard autonomy works at Mars: AutoNav evaluated 88% of the rover's first-Mars-year traverse, including record drives without human review. Science Robotics →
  • Enhanced Autonomous Navigation (ENav). The driving algorithm behind Perseverance's autonomy: two-stage path selection and hazard-aware collision checking on severely limited onboard compute — a template for the Avatar Autonomy Layer. IEEE →
Previous WorkContributionMars Avatar Extension
Sheridan (1992) Formal theory of humans operating through semi-autonomous machines rather than steering them directly. Applies the operator-to-supervisor shift to a multi-agent fleet at interplanetary delay, with an explicit authority boundary.
NASA Surface Telerobotics (2013) First fully interactive crew operation of a planetary rover from space, using command sequencing rather than joystick control. Removes the near-real-time link assumption and generalizes single-rover sequencing to fleet-level goal packages.
ESA METERON / Analog-1 (2019–2022) Force-feedback supervisory telepresence validated in lunar-analog terrain with realistic latency injection. Scales the delay by two to three orders of magnitude, where feedback-driven control is no longer available at all.
Verma et al. (2023) Flight evidence that onboard autonomy works at Mars: autonomy evaluated 88% of the first-Mars-year traverse. Treats proven single-vehicle autonomy as the assumed substrate and addresses the coordination layer above it.
Enhanced Autonomous Navigation (ENav) Hazard-aware navigation demonstrated within severe flight-compute constraints. Adopted as the reference profile for per-unit local autonomy and its resource envelope.
Choi, Brunet & How (2009) Decentralized market-based task allocation (CBBA) with convergence guarantees under limited communication. Used as the basis of the task-allocation layer, extended to intermittent links and heterogeneous unit capability.
ANA Avatar XPRIZE (2022) Demonstrated operator-embodiment interfaces for physical remote presence at terrestrial latency. Retains the intent of embodiment while abandoning immersion, which the signal budget makes impossible.

Where prior work demonstrates individual pieces — supervisory control theory, crew-in-the-loop experiments at sub-second delay, autonomous traverse — the Mars Avatar Project focuses on their integration under full interplanetary latency, with specialized avatar fleets supervised from Earth.

Scientific Transparency

Limitations and Assumptions

Stated here rather than deferred, because a concept study that omits its own boundaries is not usable as scientific input.

Conceptual Architecture Only

The work specifies a structure and its interfaces. It does not specify an implementation, and no implementation exists.

No Experimental Validation

No claim has been tested in simulation, in hardware, or in the field. All performance figures are estimates.

Correlated Failures Remain

Fleet coordination does not address common-mode failures. A hazard that disables one unit may disable all of them.

Bounded Fault Space

The architecture handles faults it anticipates. Detecting the unmodelled case is stated as an open problem, not a solved one.

Verification of Learned Components

Assuring machine-learned behaviour to flight standards is unsolved in the field generally, and is not solved here.

Sim-to-Real Transfer

Results obtained in simulation are not assumed to transfer to Martian terrain, lighting, dust, or thermal conditions.

Hardware Outside Scope

Mass, power, thermal, and radiation design for the fleet units are not addressed at any level of detail.

Order-of-Magnitude Cost

Cost estimates indicate scale only and carry no procurement or programmatic weight.
