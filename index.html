System Design

System Architecture

Six stages between intent and result. Every interface is specified around one fixed condition: the one-way signal delay between Earth and Mars is 4 to 22 minutes, and no part of the chain may assume a real-time response.

01

Human Operator

Defines objectives, constraints, priorities, and acceptance criteria. Retains authority over every irreversible action.

02

Mission AI

Translates objectives into executable tasks, allocates them across the fleet, monitors execution, and manages faults within its assigned decision class.

03

Deep Space Communication

Uplink and downlink scheduled against limited windows. Traffic is prioritized by information value rather than by request order.

04

Mars Relay Network

Orbital and surface relays extend local coverage and buffer traffic between contact periods.

05

Autonomous Robotic Fleet

Specialized avatars execute tasks on the surface, respond to hazards within seconds, and negotiate reallocation among themselves.

06

Scientific Operations

Measurements, samples, and constructed infrastructure, returned with provenance and the decision record that produced them.

Authority flows downward; evidence flows back on the same delayed channel. The operator reviews outcomes and authorizes the next objective — the operator never steers. Mission AI is the layer that makes this practical: it holds the fleet's shared model of the mission and decides how work is distributed when Earth cannot be consulted in time.

Design Rationale

Why MAP?

Six properties that distinguish this architecture from single-vehicle remote operation.

Human-in-the-Loop

Every decision of consequence remains with the operator; only the steering is delegated.

Mission AI Coordination

A single coordination layer holds the shared world model and allocates work across the fleet.

Goal-Based Control

Earth transmits the objective and its constraints; the fleet determines the method on site.

Fleet Intelligence

Specialized units bid for tasks and reassign them when a unit degrades or a plan is invalidated.

Scalable Architecture

Layer interfaces are defined independently of unit count, so the fleet can grow without redesign.

Earth → Moon → Mars Roadmap

A staged verification sequence with explicit criteria for progressing from one environment to the next.

Development Sequence

Technology Roadmap

Indicative phasing. No phase is funded, scheduled, or under way; each is contingent on the results of the one before it.

2026–2030

Concept Demonstration

Simulation of the full command chain under injected latency, with reproducible benchmarks.

2030–2035

Lunar Demonstration

Supervisory command exercised on real hardware at genuine, if short, light-time delay.

2035–2045

Mars Deployment

The validated architecture applied to surface operations at full interplanetary delay.

Objective

Robotic Infrastructure

Landing pads, power, and stored consumables emplaced and maintained without a crew present.

Objective

Support for Human Missions

Routine surface work carried by supervised avatars, reserving crew time for what requires people.

Scientific Context

Related Work

The Mars Avatar Project builds on three decades of research in supervisory control, crew-operated telerobotics, and flight-proven rover autonomy.

  • Sheridan, T.B. (1992). Telerobotics, Automation, and Human Supervisory Control. MIT Press. The foundational text on supervisory control — humans working through semi-autonomous machines rather than steering them directly. The theoretical basis for the operator-to-supervisor shift at the core of this project. MIT Press →
  • NASA Surface Telerobotics (2013). Astronauts aboard the ISS remotely supervised the K10 rover at NASA Ames across four simulated mission phases — the first fully interactive operation of a planetary rover by a crew member in space, using command sequencing rather than joystick control. Paper (arXiv) →
  • ESA METERON / Analog-1 (2019–2022). A multi-year ESA campaign in which astronauts operated the Interact rover from orbit and in lunar-analog terrain on Mount Etna, testing force-feedback control under injected signal delay. ESA →
  • Verma et al. (2023). Autonomous robotics is driving Perseverance rover's progress on Mars. Science Robotics. Flight-proven evidence that onboard autonomy works at Mars: AutoNav evaluated 88% of the rover's first-Mars-year traverse, including record drives without human review. Science Robotics →
  • Enhanced Autonomous Navigation (ENav). The driving algorithm behind Perseverance's autonomy: two-stage path selection and hazard-aware collision checking on severely limited onboard compute — a template for the Avatar Autonomy Layer. IEEE →

Where prior work demonstrates individual pieces — supervisory control theory, crew-in-the-loop experiments at sub-second delay, autonomous traverse — the Mars Avatar Project focuses on their integration under full interplanetary latency, with specialized avatar fleets supervised from Earth.
